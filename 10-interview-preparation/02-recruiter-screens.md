# Recruiter screens

Module: 10-interview-preparation
Chapter: 02-recruiter-screens
Status: beta
Last reviewed: 2026-11-26
Estimated time: 1 hour

## Learning objectives

- Pass the 30-minute recruiter screen without self-inflicted wounds
- Deliver a 90-second background answer that frames you for the role
- Handle the compensation question without disqualifying yourself

## What the recruiter is screening

Recruiters are not technical, and they are not your advocate yet - they are a filter with a checklist. The screen has three passes: communication (can this person explain their work in plain sentences), logistics (location, authorization, timeline, comp expectations), and interest (did this person ask anything specific about the role). If you clear the checklist, your file goes to the hiring manager with a one-line summary the recruiter writes from your answers. That line matters: give the recruiter the words for it.

## The 90-second background answer

Prepare it word for word and rehearse it aloud. Structure:

1. One sentence of role framing: "I build agents in LangGraph, mostly customer-facing automation."
2. Two or three sentences of most recent, most relevant work, with a number: "Most recently I shipped a support agent that deflects about 30 percent of tier-one tickets, with human approval on refund actions."
3. One sentence of why this role: "I am looking for a team that treats evaluation as first-class, which is what your posting emphasized."

The number does not have to be heroic; it has to exist. Candidates who say "improved the agent" without a figure read as tourists; candidates who say "cut escalation rate from 12 to 8 percent over two months" read as operators.

What not to do: the five-minute biography, starting from your first job; the technology inventory ("I know Python, LangGraph, LlamaIndex, Chroma, pgvector..."); and anything about what the role can do for you before what you do.

## The compensation question

"What are your expectations?" is a filter question, not a negotiation. The [salary bands](../job-market/03-salary-bands.md) chapter has the real numbers by level and region. The move that works:

1. Know the band for your level before the call, from the posting (pay-transparency laws make ranges common), from [levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/), and from [builtin.com](https://www.builtin.com/) postings.
2. Answer with a range whose bottom you would genuinely accept, anchored to the band: "For this level in this market I am looking in the 170 to 220 range; where does this role sit?"
3. Do not give a single number, do not say "whatever is fair," and do not apologize for having a range.

The recruiter's actual question is "will this file waste the panel's time." A range inside the posting's band answers it; a range 50 percent above it does not, and the process ends here quietly.

## The questions to ask

Ask two, both specific. Good ones: "What does the loop look like - how many rounds, and who runs the technical ones?" and "What is the team actually shipping this quarter?" Write the answers down: the loop structure drives your preparation, and recruiter answers are the only scheduling information you will get before the loop starts.

## Common self-inflicted rejections

- Being fuzzy on authorization or notice period. Have the exact answer.
- Naming a comp number above band with no range to soften it.
- The five-minute biography that never reaches anything relevant.
- Badmouthing a current employer, even once, even jokingly. It goes in the notes.
- Missing the call. Recruiters run screens back to back; rescheduling reads as disorganization.
