# Behavioral STAR bank

Module: 10-interview-preparation
Chapter: 14-behavioral-star-bank
Status: beta
Last reviewed: 2026-11-26
Estimated time: 2 hours

## Learning objectives

- Answer behavioral questions in the STAR shape panels can score
- Have a real story ready for each of the recurring question families
- Cut the two failure modes: the epic and the theory answer

## The STAR discipline

Situation, Task, Action, Result - two minutes, with 70 percent of the time on Action and Result. The epic (five minutes of context) and the theory answer ("I always believe in...") are the two ways this round gets failed. Write your stories, rehearse them aloud, and time them.

The bank below is the thirty questions that actually recur, grouped into families. Prepare one story per family, then map leftovers onto the nearest one - most "different" questions are a family member in disguise.

## Ownership and shipping

1. "Tell me about a project you shipped end to end." Use a [portfolio project](15-project-stories.md) with real numbers.
2. "Something you shipped that you would do differently now." Real regret, specific fix, no self-flagellation.
3. "A time you took ownership beyond your role." Not heroics: you saw the gap, you covered it, you told the team.
4. "A project that failed." Cost, cause, the durable change - the [rejection autopsy](25-rejection-autopsy.md) shape, pointed at yourself.

## Judgment and decisions

5. "A technical decision you got wrong." The refund-gate-before-eval story shape: you chose the fast path, the eval gap bit you, you installed the gate.
6. "A time you disagreed with a senior decision." Evidence, escalation once, then implementation in good faith.
7. "A decision you made with incomplete data." State the assumption, the reversible call, the check you set.
8. "A time you pushed back on scope." The non-goals list as a spoken story.

## Collaboration

9. "A conflict with a teammate." Task-level conflict, resolved by criteria not volume; working relationship preserved.
10. "Working with a difficult stakeholder." You converted them with a demo or a number, not a meeting.
11. "Explaining something technical to a non-technical audience." Use explaining the eval baseline to a product manager.
12. "A time you asked for help." Early, specific, and what you learned - not asking for help is the red flag, not the asking.
13. "Giving difficult feedback." Direct, private, about the work.
14. "Receiving difficult feedback." The correction you took and changed behavior on.

## Agent-specific (they are behavioral now)

15. "A time an agent you built did something unexpected in production." The loop or the hallucinated argument, from the [trace chapter](11-trace-debugging-round.md), as a story.
16. "How do you decide when an agent decision needs a human?" Risk of the action, reversibility, dollar threshold - a policy, not a feeling.
17. "A time you had to defend the cost of an eval investment." Frame: the regression it caught, the outage it prevented.
18. "A prompt or model change that broke something." The CI-gate story - this one wins loops.

## Pressure and change

19. "A deadline you hit by cutting the right thing." Cut scope, never verification.
20. "Competing priorities from two teams." You sequenced by user impact and told both parties the plan.
21. "A time you were wrong in public." Standing corrected gracefully - the retro or the incident channel.
22. "Learning something fast under pressure." A framework you did not know, the docs you read, the working demo in a week - use a module from this curriculum, honestly framed as learning, if you lack a work story.

## Integrity

23. "A time you found a problem in someone else's work." Private, specific, fix-first.
24. "A time you had to say no." Scope, safety, or integrity - pick one where the no held.
25. "A mistake that cost the team." Own it fully; the story is the aftermath.

## Motivation and fit

26. "Why agentic AI?" A product answer, not a hype answer: the delight of systems that plan; the engineering problem of reliability.
27. "Why this company?" Something only true of them - a shipped product, an engineering blog post, the eval culture. Vague here is fatal.
28. "Where do you want to be in three years?" Senior on agent reliability, or building the eval practice - concrete, plausible, not "your job."
29. "What kind of manager brings out your best?" Honest, one clause, positive framing.
30. "What do you do when you are stuck?" The ladder: re-read prerequisites, diff against a working reference, ask with a minimal repro - the [support chapter's](../../SUPPORT.md) discipline, told as a personal habit.

## How to rehearse

Pick twelve of the thirty (one per family you are weakest in). Write each as six bullets, rehearse each aloud in two minutes, three times. Stop when the story shape is automatic and the words are not - memorized scripts read as memorized.
