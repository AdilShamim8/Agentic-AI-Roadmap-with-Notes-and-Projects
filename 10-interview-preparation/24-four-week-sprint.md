# Four-week sprint

Module: 10-interview-preparation
Chapter: 24-four-week-sprint
Status: beta
Last reviewed: 2026-11-26
Estimated time: 4 weeks

## Learning objectives

- Run the four weeks from "curriculum finished" to "offers in hand" on a daily calendar
- Sequence applications, prep, and mock interviews so the hard rounds land when you are ready
- Keep the pipeline full enough that negotiation has leverage

## The shape of the month

Weeks one and two build the machine: materials, question banks, the first mock cycle. Week three runs the loops: screens and onsites, sequenced so the companies you care most about come second, not first - the first loop is a rehearsal you get paid in experience to run. Week four converts: offers, negotiation on the [timeline discipline](19-negotiation.md), the decision. Apply broadly in week one even though interviews land in weeks three and four; the calendar gap is the pipeline.

Assume 2 to 3 hours per weekday and 6 on one weekend day. If you are working full time, stretch the same plan across eight weeks by halving the daily blocks - do not compress by dropping the mock cycle, which is the part that moves scores.

## Week 1 - the machine

- Day 1: Resume on the [bullet formula](16-resume-portfolio.md); portfolio page skeleton; GitHub [hygiene pass](17-github-hygiene.md).
- Day 2: Three primary [project stories](15-project-stories.md) written as six STAR bullets each, rehearsed aloud.
- Day 3: The remaining seven stories to one-liners; the [behavioral bank](14-behavioral-star-bank.md) - mark your weakest family and write its story.
- Day 4: Applications. Twenty postings: eight dream, eight fit, four rehearsal-tier. Tailor one paragraph per application - the only customization that measurably matters. Tracker row per company: stage, contact, next action, date.
- Day 5: The [question banks](../questions/) for modules 01-04, self-graded; note the misses.
- Weekend: Run `interview_quiz_demo.py` for the fast pass; the [design framework](05-system-design-structure.md) drilled once against a random prompt, timed, aloud.

## Week 2 - the mocks

- Day 6: [Mock technical screen](21-mock-technical-screen.md), self-run, timed to 25 minutes, twice: tool-design prompt and graph prompt. Log the two weakest moves.
- Day 7: [Mock system design](22-mock-system-design.md) against the support-agent case, then a stranger case. The six parts must fit the budget without notes.
- Day 8: [Mock behavioral](23-mock-behavioral.md) recorded on your phone; watch it at 1x once. Everyone hates this; do it anyway - the recording is where the "like" count and the hedging become visible.
- Day 9: The [trace-debugging drill](11-trace-debugging-round.md): break a demo, diagnose from the printed steps, 20 minutes.
- Day 10: Question banks modules 05-08, self-graded; the take-home kit: `.env.example`, eval CSV template, README skeleton ready to instantiate.
- Weekend: One full loop simulation, 3 hours back to back - screen, design, behavioral - with a friend as interviewer, or with the transcripts as scripts and a hard timer. Debrief 30 minutes, written.

## Week 3 - the loops

Recruiter screens land here - they were booked in week one. Daily rhythm:

- Morning block (90 minutes): prep for the specific loop - the [company research hour](18-company-research.md) split across the morning before each company's first technical round.
- Afternoon: the interviews themselves. One loop per day maximum; two rounds in a day only if one is the recruiter screen.
- Evening (30 minutes): the debrief - three lines per round: what landed, what wobbled, the fix for next time. This log is the compounding asset of the week; the [rejection autopsy](25-rejection-autopsy.md) chapter is its aggregate.

The sequencing rule: put one rehearsal-tier company's full loop first, and the dream companies' first rounds no earlier than day three of this week. Your design answer improves most between loop one and loop two - spend that improvement where it counts.

## Week 4 - the conversion

- Take-homes: run them on the [strategy discipline](12-take-home-strategy.md) - the calendar is where over-scoping actually happens, so the non-goals list is written before the first hour of code.
- Offers arriving: the [offer-decision block](20-offer-decision.md) booked as calendar time, not squeezed between calls.
- Negotiation on the discipline from chapter 19: range set in week one, level fought before number, one counter, a close-speed trade.
- Daily: the tracker review - 15 minutes; every stalled row gets an action (a follow-up email, a scheduled nudge, a withdrawal). Stalls are normal; unmanaged stalls are pipeline death.

## The failure modes of the month

- Applying to five dream companies only. The pipeline needs volume for leverage and variety for calibration; five applications is a lottery ticket, not a search.
- Skipping the mock week to "just start interviewing." The delta between loop one and loop three is the largest score improvement available in this entire module; the mock week is how you buy it before it costs an offer.
- The take-home that eats week four. The scope discipline exists precisely here; a 20-hour take-home for a company you are lukewarm on is a scheduling bug.
- Negotiating from a single offer out of exhaustion. The 48-hour pressure moves in chapter 19 work on tired candidates; the sprint calendar builds the parallelism that makes them noise.
