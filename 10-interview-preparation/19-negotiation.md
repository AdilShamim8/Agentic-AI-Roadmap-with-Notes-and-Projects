## Learning objectives

- Negotiate from the published bands instead of guesses
- Use the two levers that work in agentic AI hiring: competing processes and quiet specificity
- Handle the pressure moves recruiters deploy at close

## Ground rules from the data

The [salary bands](../job-market/03-salary-bands.md) chapter carries the numbers by level, region, and company stage. Negotiation starts from that table, not from courage. Three structural facts hold in this market:

1. Almost everything is negotiable at offer stage except the level - and the level is the thing that compounds. Fight for the level before the number.
2. The first number moves less than candidates fear and more than companies pretend. Ranges move 5 to 15 percent; equity and sign-on move more.
3. The strongest leverage is a parallel process, not a counter-offer from a current employer - companies discount those.

## The timeline discipline

Negotiation happens at two moments only: the recruiter screen (the range that keeps the file alive, from [02](02-recruiter-screens.md)) and the offer call. Between them, say nothing about money. The mistake is negotiating while still being evaluated - it converts you from candidate to problem. When processes run in parallel, sequence the onsites so the offers land within the same two weeks; the recruiter timeline pressure ("we need an answer by Friday") is real but shrinks when two companies are waiting on you.

## The offer call

The recruiter calls with a number. Do not accept, do not counter on the call. Say the three sentences: "Thank you - I am genuinely interested in this role. I want to sit with the full picture, base, equity, sign-on, and the level. Can you send the written breakdown, and can we talk tomorrow?" Then, off the call:

1. Place the offer against the band table for the level and region. If the base is below the band floor, say exactly that - "the posted band for this level in this region starts above this number" - it is a factual, unemotional correction.
2. Identify the one axis that matters most (usually base or equity, occasionally the level or a start date) and put your energy there. Multi-axis haggling reads as shopping.
3. Counter once, with a specific number and one sentence of justification tied to the band, your parallel process, or the scope as described: "Given the band and the platform scope we discussed, 245 base with the equity as offered works for me, and I can sign this week."

The close speed you offer in return ("I can sign this week") is your payment for the movement; silent counters just start a mail thread.

## The pressure moves

- "This is the top of the band." Factual reply: "Then the sign-on or the equity is where the flex lives - can we look there?" Bands constrain base, rarely the package.
- "We need an answer in 48 hours." Reasonable reply: "I can commit to a date - Friday - if the other process I am in wraps by Thursday; if it does not, I will decide Friday with the information I have." Deadlines you set are respected; deadlines you accept blindly are extended anyway.
- "What will it take?" The honest one-sentence answer with a number. "X base and I sign today" - vagueness here only prolongs the call.

## What not to do

- Bluff a competing offer that does not exist. Recruiters ask which company; the answer's absence ends the leverage and the credibility.
- Negotiate the same axis twice. If base moved and you come back for base again, you have converted a negotiation into a rerun.
- Accept verbally on the offer call "because it feels rude not to." The politeness window closes when you say yes; everything before it is expected.
- Die on perks. Remote policy matters, snack budgets do not; the ledger from [18](18-company-research.md) tells you which is which.

## The walk posture

From the field guide: be ready to walk, because the willingness is the leverage. Decide the floor before the call, write it in the fit ledger, and when the final package is above the floor, take it without one last squeeze - the company is about to become the place your next negotiation's reputation comes from.
