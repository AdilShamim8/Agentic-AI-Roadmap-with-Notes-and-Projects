# Rejection autopsy

Module: 10-interview-preparation
Chapter: 25-rejection-autopsy
Status: beta
Last reviewed: 2026-11-26
Estimated time: 45 minutes

## Learning objectives

- Read a rejection for its actual cause instead of its stated one
- Fix the six causes that account for most no-hires in agentic AI loops
- Extract signal from the rare feedback that carries any

## The stated reason and the real one

Rejection emails cite fit and headcount. The debrief that produced them cited something on a rubric line. You will rarely get the real sentence; you can usually reconstruct it from the round you felt worst in - candidates are accurate self-sensors of their weakest hour, and the debrief is disproportionately decided by exactly one below-bar round ([the landscape](01-interview-landscape.md) again: consistency beats peaks).

Run the autopsy the evening the email lands, while the rounds are fresh: one line per round - strongest moment, weakest moment, the question that got you. Three rejections with the same weakest moment is a skill gap with a name; fix the gap before the next loop.

## The six causes

1. The eval-shaped hole. The answer never mentioned how the system is measured; the design or take-home read as pre-eval instincts. This is the most common and the cheapest fix in this field: the golden set, the evaluators, the CI gate, spoken unprompted. If you finished this curriculum and stay silent on evals, you are leaving the exact differentiator it teaches unused.

2. The wrong loop prep. An agent-product loop got the platform answer or vice versa ([the two variants](01-interview-landscape.md)). The fix is the company-research hour: the stage read picks the story set.

3. The inconsistent round. One strong round, one weak one - the technical screen went well, the design round drifted, or the behavioral story disguised a failure. The fix is the mock week: every round rehearsed at least once, because unrehearsed rounds are the ones that wobble.

4. The artifact failed the audit. The take-home did not run from a fresh clone, or the GitHub profile leaked a secret or showed imported homework ([hygiene](17-github-hygiene.md)). This one stings because it is mechanical: the fresh-clone check is ten minutes, and skipping it is how strong candidates lose to their own tarball.

5. The level mismatch. Your scope stories are one level below the role's bar, and no answer quality compensates. The fix is story surgery, not more rounds: the [project stories](15-project-stories.md) chapter, rewritten around decisions and numbers at the target level - what you chose, what it cost, what you would change.

6. The bar moved. The role closed, the reorg ate the headcount, the internal candidate got it. Rejections in this category carry no signal; if two facts point to it - the process was slow, then abruptly ended, with no feedback on any technical thread - file it and spend nothing on it.

## Getting real feedback

Ask once, narrowly, in the thank-you thread to the recruiter: "If there is one round it would help me to work on, I would appreciate knowing it." Narrow ask, one round - not "why was I rejected." Recruiters sometimes relay a sentence from the debrief, and one real sentence beats a week of guessing. Ask for yourself, not to argue the decision: the engineers who interviewed you may staff the next loop at the same company, and the person who took the no gracefully gets re-interviewed; the person who lit up the thread does not.

## The re-interview rule

Companies that rejected you re-interview in 6 to 12 months, and the second loop starts from a better position: the first no cleared the fog of the unknown candidate. Log the fix for each autopsy line, and when the recruiter reappears in the cycle, the story is "I shipped the eval practice I was missing" - the strongest possible re-entry sentence this field offers.
